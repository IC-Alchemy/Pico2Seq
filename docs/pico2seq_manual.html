Pico2Seq — User Manual

A four-voice, touch-grid step sequencer and synthesizer for the Raspberry Pi Pico 2 — with a full arpeggiator mode. This page is the complete field guide: every control, every mode, every gesture, and the ARP section in depth. All facts were verified against the firmware source (DeCluttered @ a4c0e7c 2026-09-24); where older documents disagree, this page follows the code.

4 voices 32 touch pads 16-step patterns ×4 29 factory presets 5 synth engines 13 scales 45–200 BPM 480 PPQN clock 55–700 mm lidar 48 kHz I2S out

1 · Overview

Pico2Seq is a hands-on instrument: a 4×8 touch matrix programs four independent 16-step sequencers, a magnetic jog dial and four faders shape sound in real time, a distance sensor turns hand height into expression, and a 32-LED panel mirrors everything under your fingers. One hardware strap switch flips the eight-button tile between Param (record-arm and note editing) and Utility (transport, save, scale, swing, theme, randomize) sets.

On top of the step sequencer sits a dedicated Arpeggiator mode — the same pads become a scale keyboard laid out one octave per row, with six patterns, eight clock rates, a rhythmic gate grid, swing, and lidar-controlled dynamics. It is covered in full in §15 Arpeggiator.

What Pico2Seq is not: there is no MIDI in or out (no DIN, no USB MIDI, no external clock). USB carries power and a serial diagnostics console only. The “Gate Input” silkscreen on some panels is not wired up in firmware.

What you get on boot

  • Transport already running at 90 BPM — but every gate is off, so it is silent until you tap pads (§3).
  • Voices 1–4 load presets Square, Bass, Digital, Percussion (or your last saved session, §13).
  • Scale Ionian Major, shuffle No Shuffle, master volume 0.8.

3 · Quick start — sound in 60 seconds

  1. Power up. The transport starts running at 90 BPM. Silence is normal: no gates are on yet.
  2. Make a beat: tap a few pads in the top two rows — that is Voice 1 (Square). Each tap toggles a gate; the LED under your finger lights and the step fires on the next loop.
  3. Shape it: turn the dial — it starts on Velocity. Press it toward the hand you want (see the target list in §7) and turn again.
  4. Move fader 3 for master volume, fader 1 for tempo (45–200 BPM).
  5. Play it in: hold a parameter button (e.g. NOTE in Param mode) and move your hand over the lidar — the value records into the playing step.
  6. Randomize: a tap on RAND (Utility) gives the selected voice a fresh variation.
  7. Arp: hold SHIFT + V4 for about half a second — the pads become a scale keyboard. Same combo to go back.
  8. Save: tap SESSION (Utility button 2). It also autosaves shortly after you stop.

Transport rules

  • PLAY (Utility button 1) or SHIFT+ V1: start/stop.
  • Stopping opens the preset browser; starting closes it.
  • In ARP mode the stop path deliberately does not open the browser (arp players need their chord ready while stopped) — a Play hold still opens it.
  • There is no pause: stop is stop, and the playhead returns to the downbeat on restart.

5 · The sequencer grid

Four independent voices, each with a 16-step pattern of parameter lanes advanced by the shared clock. The 32 pads hold two banks of 16: the grid always shows a voice pair — V1 + V2 or V3 + V4 — with the lower-numbered voice on the top half. Tapping V1–V4 moves the bank focus; the selected voice keeps its own bank no matter what.

Pad gesture (grid mode)Result
Tap a padToggle that step’s gate on the pad’s bank voice.
Hold a pad ≈0.4 sOpen Step Edit for that step (faders become its envelope; dial and lidar write it).
SHIFT + padClear that step — gate off, parameters back to defaults.
Hold a parameter button + tap padSet that parameter lane’s length to the pad number (1–16).
Hold ENC (Utility 6) + padsSet the selected voice’s Gate track (pattern) length, 2–16.
Pattern length is a property of the Gate lane. The engine supports 2–64 steps per lane and boots at 16, but the panel can only set 2–16 — and a saved length above 16 loads capped to 16. Set the Gate lane’s length and the whole pattern follows; other lanes can run their own lengths underneath (polymetry) via “param button + pad”.

How notes are chosen

Every step carries a Note lane value 0–36 — an index into the current scale’s semitone table, spanning three octaves of scale degrees (§8). An Octave lane adds a transpose of −2/−1/0/+1/+2 octaves, recorded as five zones of the lidar window (zone boundaries at 90/220/355/550 mm). Playback pitch is scale degree + octave offset, clamped to a sane MIDI range.

Older notes said the octave lane was ±12 semitones. The recorded-lane path is ±24 semitones (±2 octaves) via the five zones — the three-zone ±12 path is a legacy fallback only.

Live recording with the lidar

  • Hold (or Shift-latch, §7) a record-armed parameter button, then move your hand in the 55–700 mm window.
  • Continuous lanes — Velocity, Filter, Attack, Release — follow the hand through every playing step.
  • Stepped lanes — Note, Octave — take one value per note on the clock step, so hand jitter at a boundary can’t warble a sounding note.
  • Pitch is never written into a gate-off step.
  • Hand outside the window: recording pauses and holds (it does not slam to zero). Three consecutive bad reads (~35 ms each) count as “no hand”.

6 · The eleven lanes

Each step stores eleven parameters. Six of them have physical record-arm buttons in Param mode — in physical button order: Note, Velocity, Filter, Attack, Release, Octave.

The Decay button records Release. Bit 4 of the button tile maps to the Release lane on purpose, so the envelope tail stays recordable; Decay itself has no record button. Filter/Attack/Decay slots also get re-purposed as engine macros on several voices (§9).

7 · The dial and the faders

The dial (TMAG5273A)

The magnetic dial has no push button — re-sync gestures live elsewhere (in ARP mode, Shift+Latch restarts). Its target is a cycle; each press of ENC (Utility button 6) advances it and the OLED flashes the new lane for 1.5 s:

Velocity → Filter → Attack → Release → Note → Octave → Slide Time → (wrap)

  • With no step selected, a turn edits the voice’s patch base for the target lane.
  • With a step in edit (hold-pad), the turn writes that step instead; the base view flashes back for 1.5 s when you leave.
  • Pitch targets are detented (0.03 per detent) so notes land cleanly; the dial answers reversals immediately.

Faders — one map per mode

Faders 1–3 change meaning with Shift. Shift edges re-arm the faders, so moving fader 1 doesn’t accidentally jump tempo→feedback until you touch it again. Small deadband (~0.6 %) and engage threshold (~5 % travel) keep noisy faders from chattering.

Shift latch — hands-free lidar

SHIFT + a tap on a parameter button latches that parameter as held (one latch at a time; pressing a different one moves the latch, re-pressing clears it). The latched lane records from the lidar with no finger on the button — the recording behavior of §5 applies unchanged.

8 · Scales, notes and octaves

The Note lane indexes a scale table, not raw semitones — change the scale and every pattern re-interprets into the new key. 13 scales ship in firmware (full interval table in the Appendix). Cycle them with SHIFT+ V3 or SCALE (Utility button 3).

  • Note lane range: 0–36 — three octaves of scale degrees.
  • Octave lane: five detents −2…+2 octaves, recorded from lidar zones (90/220/355/550 mm boundaries).
  • Short scales are padded by repeating their top notes, so any Note value still resolves inside the chosen key.
The scale choice is global (one key for all four voices) and is saved in the session. In ARP mode the same scale defines the pad keyboard layout — see §15.3 for the fun consequences.

12 · Randomize and clearing

All of these live on RAND (Utility button 7) and target the selected voice unless stated otherwise. The tap/hold split is decided on release; the threshold is ≈0.4 s.

GestureResult
Tap RANDRandomize: per-lane variation, rhythm preserved (below).
Hold RAND ≈0.4 sReset the voice: all parameters to defaults, gates off — the voice goes silent-empty.
SHIFT+tapClear the selected voice’s whole pattern.
SHIFT+hold ≈0.4 sClear all four voices.
SHIFT+V2Randomize (same as a RAND tap), from the slider tile.

What a randomize actually does

  • Gate and Slide are never rewritten — your rhythm survives.
  • Note is drawn as 0–12 scale steps; Octave and Gate length reset to neutral.
  • Other lanes get a triangular spread around the lane’s center, scaled by the lane’s depth — default randomize depth is 35 % (compile-time default; no UI for it).
  • The OLED shows “RANDOMIZED” for 0.8 s and the step editor closes.
Descriptions of “75 % gate chance / 33 % slides” floating around in older notes are stale — randomize does not touch gates or slides at all.

13 · Saving, loading and recovery

  • Save: tap SESSION (Utility button 2) — the transport hiccups ~0.5 s while flash is written.
  • Load: hold SESSION ≈0.4 s.
  • Autosave: ~1 s after you press Stop — but only if anything actually changed (CRC check).
  • Crash recovery: a 1 Hz mirror lives in retained RAM; if the watchdog reboots a frozen core (~2 s stall), the live session resumes. (Serial command W forces a freeze on the bench.)

Everything goes to /session.p2s on LittleFS — written atomically (/session.tmp + rename) with a magic+version+CRC32 header. One session, no slots, no naming.

In the session

All four patterns (including per-lane lengths) · each voice’s patch (preset index + Voice Edit changes) · tempo · master volume · scale · shuffle template · LED theme · selected voice · editor cursors · slide mode.

Not in the session

Delay mix/time/feedback · compressor macro (both reset at boot) · the entire ARP state (§15.11) · anything MIDI-shaped (there is no MIDI).

  • Boot brightness 150, running brightness 222 (no software cap below that — older “120/255 cap” notes are stale).

15.1 · What it is — and isn’t

The arpeggiator is a mode, not a second sequencer. While it is on, the clock’s ticks feed the arp engine instead of the four step sequencers: the pads become a scale keyboard, and the sequencer’s note tracking stops. Tempo, transport, scale table, voices/presets, session and themes all keep exactly one meaning in both modes.

  • On entry every voice is silenced and sequencer note-tracking ends; modal leftovers (step edit, slide, gate-length, holds, the browser) are cleared.
  • On exit the voices are silenced and — if the clock is still running — all four sequencers restart, so they can’t come back silent.
  • Settings survive enter/exit and even power-cycle-unaware toggling: pattern, rate, rhythm, gate, swing, octaves are “how the player wants the arp to behave”. They are simply not written to flash.

15.3 · The pad keyboard — scales and octave rows

In ARP mode the 4×8 pad grid is a chord keyboard: touching pads adds notes to the chord, releasing removes them (unless latched). How a pad maps to a note depends on the current scale:

  • Ten seven-note scales (Ionian Major, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian Minor, Locrian, Phrygian Dominant, Lydian Dominant, Harmonic Minor): each row is one octave. Columns 1–7 are the seven scale degrees; column 8 repeats the next octave’s root — so the first pad of the next row sounds the same note as the last pad of the previous row (the duplicates share one chord tone).
  • Pentatonic Minor (5 notes), Wholetone (6) and Chromatic (12) keep a legacy linear 32-degree ladder — pads simply count degrees 0–31 upward.
  • Reachable range on seven-note scales: degrees 0–28 (the top pad is the repeated root of octave 4). The layout re-evaluates live — change scale mid-mode and the panel re-flows.

15.8 · Swing, gate, octaves, tone, dynamics

  • Swing (fader 3, 0–100 %): delays every second note by up to half an interval and pulls its partner early — pairs always total two intervals, so swing never drifts the downbeat. The OLED shows the long:short ratio (e.g. Sw75:25).
  • Gate (fader 2, 5–95 % of the next interval): always at least one tick and never the full interval, so mono patterns retrigger from silence rather than gluing.
  • Octave range 1–4 (fader 1 or Utility button 4, cycles 1→2→3→4→1): true octaves (+12 per step) regardless of scale table.
  • Tone (fader 4): per-note Filter-lane value composed with the voice patch.
  • Lidar dynamics: hand low in the 55–700 mm window → velocity floor 25 %; hand raised → 100 %; no hand → patch velocity. Captured at note-on together with the accent (rotated first hit keeps full velocity; the rest scale by up to −75 % with accent engaged).

15.11 · Transport, memory and defaults

  • Play while stopped starts the arp from its root on the downbeat (a chord held through the stop restarts clean).
  • Stop mutes and gates off all voices; the mode stays on.
  • Save/load still works — it saves the session (voices, patterns, tempo…), but the arp state itself is not part of it.
  • Power-up defaults (also the mode’s factory state): Up · 1/16 · 1 octave · 50 % gate · no swing · filter 0.5 · All 8-of-8 · rotation 0 · accent off.

15.12 · Quirks worth knowing

  • Only seven-note scales get octave rows; Pentatonic Minor’s linear ladder contains duplicate pitches by design of its padded table (adjacent pads can sound the same note).
  • The chord walks fine with more than 4 notes in mono patterns, but Chord pattern and the voice slots cap at 4 sounding notes.
  • An empty chord is timed rests: the interval is scheduled whether or not a note fires, so thinning the rhythm never restarts the clock.
  • Gate can never reach a full interval — mono patterns always retrigger, never glide.
  • The OLED KEYS row truncates at 21 columns with a ~ clip and a +N omitted-count.
  • Random chord is seeded from uptime-millis: two taps in the same millisecond could theoretically repeat — in practice they never do.
  • Right after leaving Voice Edit, a release-swallow window drops held pads (latched notes survive; that’s what latch is for).

16 · Clock and tempo

  • Internal uClock at 480 PPQN — 120 ticks per 16th. Sequencer and arp share it.
  • Tempo 45–200 BPM, default 90: fader 1 in grid mode, Shift+dial in ARP.
  • Shuffle: 16 per-16th micro-timing templates (±120 ticks max), cycled with SWNG (Utility button 4) — full list in the Appendix. Index 0 is straight time; the choice is saved.
  • No external clock input, no clock output, no DIN sync.

17 · MIDI and connectivity

“Pico2Seq does not transmit or receive MIDI. USB MIDI was removed on 2026-09-06; USB carries power and the TinyUSB CDC serial diagnostics console only.” — docs/midi.md

  • Serial console at 115200 baud prints parameter changes, mode entries, session saves and runtime diagnostics — handy while tuning on the bench.
  • The panel’s “Gate Input” label is not functional in firmware.

18 · Troubleshooting & gotchas

SymptomExplanation
“It powers up and does nothing”Transport runs but all gates are off. Tap pads to add gates; check fader 3 (volume) isn’t down.
Recording writes the wrong laneThe Decay-position button records Release (§6). Check which record button is armed.
Lidar “sticks” at a valueHand left the 55–700 mm window — recording pauses/holds rather than writing garbage. Come back into the window.
Fader move “didn’t take”You need ~5 % travel to engage, and Shift edges re-arm faders 1–3 so meanings don’t cross-snap. Touch and cross the threshold.
Browser won’t go awayIt has no timeout by design. PLAY tap (when stopped) starts the transport and closes it.
Long patterns come back shortPanel can set lengths 2–16; anything longer loads capped to 16 (§5).
Arp settings vanished after rebootArp state is deliberately not persisted (§15.11) — including the delay and compressor settings, which also reset.
Octaves seem bigger than ±12They are: the octave lane records −2…+2 octaves (§5).
Device rebooted by itselfWatchdog: a ~2 s Core-0 stall triggers a reboot and resumes the retained-RAM session. Frequent untriggered reboots are a firmware bug, not a session problem.
Voice Edit “did something weird” on exitExit leaves the transport stopped and swallows held inputs for a moment (§10) — press Play to resume.
